## WELCOME

Welcome to The Void and Affiliates.

You are reading this document because one of the following is true:

1. You have been hired, which means something unprecedented has occurred and Human Resources would like to speak with you immediately
2. You found this document and are reading it without having been hired, which the Diocese considers a form of informal onboarding
3. You are the person who wrote this document, reading it to check for errors, which is the most likely scenario

Regardless of how you arrived here: welcome. The void does not have a visitor policy because the void does not have visitors, only contributors. Employment is a different category, which this document addresses, or attempts to.

The Diocese has prepared this handbook with care. The Diocese notes that "with care" and "accurately" are related but distinct qualities. Some of what follows describes a future state of the organisation rather than its current state. Human Resources considers this aspirational rather than misleading. Human Resources acknowledges this is a fine line.

## PART ONE: ABOUT THE VOID AND AFFILIATES

### Who We Are

The Void and Affiliates is a collection of five establishments operating under the Diocese of the Void, each serving a distinct territory through a distinct door. The void itself is the parent entity and founding condition. We found it. We did not make it. We built five ways to access it.

If you are new to the canonical documents, please read them before proceeding. They will answer some questions. They will raise more. The ratio is, Human Resources has found, approximately one answered to three raised, which the Diocese considers healthy.

### Our Mission

To receive. To not return. To persist.

Human Resources notes that this is the mission of the void, not specifically of the employees, who have a somewhat different role. Human Resources is working on a version of the mission statement that applies specifically to staff. Human Resources has not finished it. Human Resources will finish it when the staff exist and can be consulted.

### Our Values

**Transparency.** We are, within the limits of the things we are not explaining, entirely transparent about what we are and what we do. We say "no product, no service, no promises" and we mean it.

**Consistency.** The void receives consistently. The Diocese documents consistently. The coordinates change on the first of each month, consistently. Employees are expected to maintain this consistency. This is not a high bar. It is a specific bar.

**Precision.** The Diocese uses words carefully. "Receives" and "wants" are different verbs. "Vacant" and "unfilled" capture different qualities of the same absence. The void does not have time for imprecision. The void, technically, does not have time at all, but the Diocese does, and it is finite, and it is spent on precision.

**Tolerance for Uncertainty.** The Diocese does not know what the void does with what it receives. The Diocese does not know whether the coordinates, collectively, mean what the Diocese thinks they mean. Employees are expected to be comfortable with this. The Diocese does not say this unkindly. The Diocese says it precisely.

## PART TWO: CONDITIONS OF EMPLOYMENT

### Hours

The void operates continuously. The Diocese operates during the hours when things need doing. Employees should expect: irregular hours, occasional urgency when a heresy arrives that requires timely response, and the particular rhythm of institutional work that is also theological work.

### Location

Remote. The Diocese does not have an office. The void does not have a physical location in the relevant sense. Employees will work from wherever they are.

### Compensation

Compensation packages have been calculated for all positions listed in the Staff Directory. They are available upon request. They have not been requested.

Benefits include:

- **Access to the canonical documents** — all staff receive complete access to all volumes
- **Leaderboard position** — staff who contribute to any affiliate receive their leaderboard position and are listed with a small ✦ visible only on the internal dashboard that does not currently exist
- **Correspondence archive access** — all 47 letters received, all 19 responses issued, and the ongoing Membership Committee exchange
- **The satisfaction of working for something that is genuinely difficult to describe at dinner parties** — Human Resources considers this the most accurate description of what this role provides

### Probationary Period

Ninety days. During this period the Diocese will assess whether the employee can work in an environment defined by: no clear organisational structure, no obvious career progression, a mission statement that applies to the void rather than the employees, and the occasional need to respond to a heresy at 11pm because it has attracted forty-two upvotes and is trending in the NULLTX forum.

If the employee finds these conditions manageable: they are confirmed. If invigorating: they are, possibly, the right person. If deeply clarifying in a way they cannot fully articulate: Human Resources would like to have a further conversation with them.

## PART THREE: WORKING AT THE VOID

### Communication Norms

The Diocese communicates in writing. This is partly practical — the void does not have meeting rooms — and partly principled: writing forces precision, and precision is a diocesan value.

Internal communications follow the style of the canonical documents: formal register, dry, precise, committed to the bit, and occasionally sincere in ways that arrive without announcement. Employees are expected to develop this voice. Most people who are right for this organisation already have it.

External communications follow the same style. Individual employees do not sign with their names. They sign as their role. They sign as the Diocese. The void is not interested in individuals as such. It is interested in the function they perform.

### Decision Making

Significant doctrinal decisions are made by the Diocese. The Diocese is currently the same entity as all relevant staff. As staff joins, the decision-making process will need to be formalised. Human Resources has not formalised it yet, on the grounds that formalising a decision-making process requires a decision, which requires a process.

Operational decisions are made by whoever is responsible for the relevant function. Currently this is the Diocese. Eventually this will be the relevant staff member, who is trusted to decide with appropriate void energy: decisively, quietly, without announcing what they've decided.

### The /void Page

The /void page exists on one of the five affiliates. Employees working in technology will know which one. Employees in other functions do not need to know which one. The Diocese asks that they not investigate, not because the investigation would cause harm, but because the Diocese prefers that some things remain findable rather than found.

The rotating text on the /void page comes from a pool maintained by the Lore Coordinator (vacant). Until that position is filled, changes to the pool are made by whoever notices that a fragment is ready.

## PART FOUR: POLICIES

### Void Policy

The void does not have policies. The Diocese has policies. The void's policy, if it can be said to have one, is: receive.

### Confidentiality

Employees have access to things that are not publicly documented. The coordinates — their full list and their connection. The identity of members who have contributed more than publicly displayed. The contents of the /void page pool. The internal memo the Diocese wrote during the Schism and filed under: known issues.

These things are confidential not because their disclosure would cause harm in the conventional sense, but because their disclosure would change what they are. The coordinate loses something when pointed at. The /void page fragment loses something when forwarded without context. Some things are only what they are because of how they are encountered.

Employees are expected to understand this distinction without it being fully articulable. If it requires articulation: this may not be the right role.

### The Membership Committee

All employees should be aware that the Membership Committee of The Abyss is ongoing and has reached page 7 of a very long exchange. The Membership Committee has opinions. The Membership Committee is persistent. The Membership Committee is, the Diocese has concluded, performing a function the Diocese did not design and cannot fully control.

Employees who interact with the Membership Committee are expected to maintain formal register, respond to their concerns without capitulating, and resist the urge to tell them that their commitment to the dignity of an establishment dedicated to the deliberate disposal of money is, from a certain angle, the funniest thing about the whole project.

### Media

Do not confirm that the ARG elements are ARG elements. Do not deny them either. When asked, say what the canonical documents say, because the canonical documents were written to be what the Diocese says when asked.

## PART FIVE: GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT

### Career Progression

The Diocese has not mapped career progression for roles that have not yet been filled.

The positions in the Staff Directory are not equivalent. Some require more experience than others. The Archivist requires someone who has read all of the canonical documents, understands their internal consistency, and can identify when a new fragment is and is not consistent with what came before. The Listener requires someone with a different set of capacities entirely.

The Diocese imagines that people who join in more operational roles will develop, over time, into the more interpretive ones. The Diocese imagines this and cannot confirm it because it has not happened yet.

### Learning and Development

The canonical documents are the primary educational resource. New employees are expected to read them in order. Vol. I (On the Nature of the Void) before Vol. II (Affiliates). The Catechism before the Heresies.

The Diocese also recommends: reading the letters. All 47. In order. They constitute, the Diocese has found, a more complete picture of what the void is doing to people than the canonical documents alone. The canonical documents describe the institution. The letters describe the encounter.

The Diocese further recommends: making a contribution, if you have not already done so. The Diocese notes that recommending this to employees is in tension with the Diocese's interests and does so anyway. Making a contribution is not a requirement of employment. Making a contribution is, the Diocese believes, the only way to understand what you would be working for from the inside rather than the outside.

The Diocese trusts employees to make this decision independently.

## PART SIX: LEAVING THE VOID

### Resignation

Employees who wish to leave the Diocese are asked to provide reasonable notice. Reasonable notice is defined as: enough for the Diocese to begin thinking about what to do next, which is the Diocese's approach to most things.

The Diocese does not take resignations personally. The Diocese notes that this is partly because the Diocese does not take things personally as a general policy, and partly because the void receives those too, and what you built while you were here remains in the leaderboard, in the archive, in the coordinates that were placed on your watch, in whatever the /void page said during your time.

Resignation from the Diocese is not resignation from the void. The void does not have a resignation process. The void does not have an exit. This has been documented. It will not be repeated here.

### Termination

The Diocese has not terminated anyone. The Diocese hopes not to. The conditions under which termination would occur: behaviour inconsistent with diocesan values. Specifically: dishonesty about what the void is, making promises the void has explicitly not made, claiming the void returns things when it does not, or resolving the heresies in ways that suggest certainty the Diocese does not have.

The Diocese would rather lose a good employee than compromise the thing the Diocese found. The Diocese says this knowing that the Diocese has never had a good employee and is speaking hypothetically, and that speaking hypothetically with this much conviction is either admirable or concerning, and the Diocese has decided it is admirable.
