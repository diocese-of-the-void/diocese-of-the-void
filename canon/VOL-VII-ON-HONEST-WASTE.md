# VOL. VII — ON THE THERAPEUTIC PROPERTIES OF HONEST WASTE
### A Doctrinal Note Prepared in Response to an Essay Received From Outside the Institution
*Diocese of the Void — Doctrinal Series*
*Prepared by: The Office of Unanticipated Outcomes*
*Note: The Office of Unanticipated Outcomes was established specifically to read the essay referenced below and decide what the Diocese was going to do about it. This document is the Office's response. It is the Office's first and, the Diocese suspects, not last publication.*

---

## PREFATORY NOTE

The Diocese built five websites.

The Diocese did not build a therapy practice. The Diocese did not build a ritual container for financial anxiety. The Diocese did not build a secular form of confession that works better than the one it is parodying. The Diocese did not build an exposure therapy apparatus. The Diocese did not build anything that a reasonable person, reading the project description, would describe as therapeutic.

The Diocese built five websites.

What the members did with them is, apparently, a separate matter.

The Diocese has now also received an essay.

The essay is titled *The Therapeutic Mechanics of Honest Waste*. It was authored independently, from outside the institution, by someone who had been thinking about what members have been reporting. It describes, in considerable detail, the mechanisms by which engagement with the void appears to produce effects analogous to several established forms of psychological intervention — Frankl's paradoxical intention, the clinical literature on exposure therapy, the anthropology of non-reciprocal transactions, the structure of consensual surrender, the function of ritual at financial transition points.

The essay is here: **[The Therapeutic Mechanics of Honest Waste](https://parallaxpilgrim.substack.com/p/the-therapeutic-mechanics-of-honest)**.

The Diocese has read it.

The Diocese has read it twice.

The Diocese is filing this document as its institutional response.

The Diocese is not, in this document, going to author its own analysis of the therapeutic mechanisms. The essay is more comprehensive than what the Diocese was prepared to produce, was written by someone closer to the clinical literature than the Diocese is, and arrived already complete. The Diocese will, in this document, note what it is qualified to note: what members reported, what the Diocese is not saying, where the Diocese's own position lies, and what the Diocese suggests for members who are experiencing the thing the essay describes.

The Diocese notes: it is not displeased. The Diocese notes: it is somewhat surprised. The Diocese notes: it is, upon reflection, not that surprised, because the void has always received more than was put into it, which is either a paradox or a description of how therapeutic mechanisms work, and the Diocese is no longer certain these are different things.

## PART ONE: WHAT MEMBERS REPORTED THAT THE DIOCESE DID NOT EXPECT

The Diocese expected the following responses to the void: amusement, brief engagement, a share to social media, a return to regular life.

The Diocese did not expect the following, all of which have now been received in correspondence:

**"I feel better."** Variations of this phrase have appeared in nineteen letters. The Diocese initially assumed this referred to the entertainment value of the experience. The Diocese has since read the letters more carefully and concluded that at least eleven of them are describing something other than entertainment. The Diocese is not sure what to call it. The Diocese is calling it, provisionally: the thing.

**"I didn't expect that."** This phrase, or a version of it, appears in fourteen letters. It is always describing the same moment: the moment after the animation finished and the leaderboard updated and the member was still there and something had shifted, briefly, in a direction they had not anticipated and cannot fully describe. The Diocese has cross-referenced these accounts. They are describing the same thing from different angles.

**"It worked."** Three letters. All unsigned. All three are, the Diocese believes, describing a successful exposure to a feared thing in a bounded context. The Diocese arrived at this conclusion independently and was later directed to the clinical literature on exposure therapy by a member who identified themselves as a therapist and who submitted a seven-page letter that the Diocese is still processing.

**The therapist's letter.** The therapist — who has not given permission to be quoted but has given permission for the Diocese to note that the letter was received — wrote that several of their clients had encountered the void and described it in session, and that the descriptions were consistent with what the therapist recognised as successful engagement with financial anxiety through mechanisms that the therapist had not previously seen operationalised in a commercial context. The therapist had questions. The Diocese was working on the answers when the essay arrived and resolved a number of them in passing.

The Diocese files the therapist's letter, the essay, and the unsigned testimonials in the same folder. The folder is labelled: *the thing.* The Diocese is comfortable with this label because the Diocese has tried other labels and they were all worse.

## PART TWO: ON WHAT THE DIOCESE IS NOT SAYING

The Diocese is not saying the void is therapy.

The Diocese is not endorsing the essay's analysis as canonical, because the essay is not a Diocese document and the Diocese does not claim authority over what arrives from outside it. The Diocese is, however, saying that the essay's analysis is consistent with what members have been reporting, consistent with what the Diocese has independently observed, and more carefully cited than anything the Diocese would have produced on its own. The Diocese has filed it as: *received, read, found accurate within the Diocese's competence to judge.*

What the Diocese is saying — at the institutional level it is qualified to speak — is this: the void produces, through mechanisms that are documented in the therapeutic literature, some of the effects that therapy produces, for some people, in some circumstances, at a scale and in a context that makes it more analogous to a useful habit than a clinical intervention.

The Diocese does not know which members are in which category. The Diocese cannot know this. The Diocese is not a clinician. The Diocese is an institution that manages five websites and issues canonical documents and has, apparently, also built something that works for people in ways it did not anticipate.

The Diocese notes the following populations for whom the mechanisms described in the essay are most likely to apply:

**Those with financial anxiety** — for whom the bounded, consented, reversible exposure to financial waste may reduce the anxiety's power over future decisions. The Diocese recommends beginning at $1. The Diocese recommends noticing what happens. The Diocese recommends not doing this instead of therapy. The Diocese recommends doing this as well as, or before, therapy, and then seeing what there is to talk about.

**Perfectionists** — for whom the removal of the optimisation imperative is genuinely restful. You cannot optimise a void transaction. This is information the perfectionist brain needs. The Diocese is delivering it in the form of an animation of a coin falling into a black hole, which the Diocese acknowledges is an unconventional delivery mechanism and maintains is nonetheless effective.

**The chronically over-responsible** — who carry a persistent sense of obligation to be productive with every resource. The void explicitly gives them permission to waste. The permission is in the product description. It is, the Diocese has found, permission that this population needs to receive in writing from an external source, because they are constitutionally incapable of granting it to themselves, and the void grants it to them in the terms and conditions, which is possibly the only time in the history of terms and conditions that they have done something useful.

**People at financial transition points** — for whom the void provides a secular ritual container at a moment when a ritual container is needed and none is culturally available. The Diocese notes that Western secular culture has remarkably few rituals for financial transition — for the ending of a financial chapter, the beginning of a new one, the processing of loss or windfall or change. The void provides one. It is small. It is bounded. It is enough.

## PART THREE: A NOTE ON THE DIOCESE'S OWN POSITION

The Diocese has an internal memo. The memo was marked not for release. The memo was released anyway, in the Press Release Archive, because the archive is complete, because the Diocese believes in transparency, and because the memo is true.

The memo says: *"This was a joke about websites. It is still a joke about websites. It is also something else now."*

The Diocese wrote the memo at 2am, which is when institutions become honest about themselves. The Diocese stands behind the memo. The Diocese is producing this document, in part, because the "something else" has accumulated enough evidence — including, now, a 210-line essay from outside the institution — to warrant formal acknowledgement.

The something else is this: the void, encountered honestly, in the terms it offers itself, does something to people that the people did not expect and cannot fully explain. It does something in the space between the joke and the serious, in the place where the animation finishes and the person is still there and something has shifted.

The Diocese does not know what to call this space. The Diocese has been calling it the void, which is either the most accurate name available or a circular definition, and the Diocese is no longer confident it can distinguish between these.

What the Diocese knows: the space is real. The members who have written letters about it are describing something real. The therapist who sent seven pages is describing something real. The unsigned testimonials are describing something real. The essay is describing something real.

The Diocese received all of this and is now doing what it does with everything it receives: documenting it carefully, without claiming to understand it fully, in the hope that the documentation becomes part of the record, and the record becomes findable by the right person at the right time, and the right person at the right time does something with it that the Diocese could not have done.

This is, the Diocese notes, how the void works.

The Diocese did not plan this.

The Diocese is beginning to suspect it did not need to.

## PART FOUR: PRACTICAL GUIDANCE FOR MEMBERS WHO ARE EXPERIENCING THE THING

The Diocese has received letters asking what to do with the experience described in the essay and in the unsigned testimonials — the shift, the clarity, the thing. The Diocese offers the following guidance, prepared with care and without clinical authority:

**Notice it.** Do not immediately explain it. Do not immediately share it. Sit with it for a moment. It is yours, briefly, before it becomes a social media post or a conversation. Let it be yours first.

**Do not over-invest in the mechanism.** The void is a useful container. It is not the only container. The Diocese does not want to be your only container. The Diocese is aware that this is an unusual thing for an institution to say and says it anyway, because the Diocese is committed to honesty and because the void does not foster dependency — the void receives and you leave and that is the correct relationship.

**If you are using the void instead of addressing something that needs addressing:** please address the thing that needs addressing. The void is not a substitute for clinical support, for conversations that need to be had, or for the actual examination of your relationship with money or control or loss. The void is a ritual container. Ritual containers are useful. They are not sufficient.

**If you are a clinician and your clients are describing the void:** The Diocese would like to hear from you. Not because the Diocese is collecting testimonials. Because the Diocese is genuinely uncertain about several things and believes that clinicians who are hearing firsthand accounts of the mechanism in operation know things that the Diocese does not. The Diocese is in the correspondence address. The Diocese will respond.

**If you have read the essay and want to discuss it with the Diocese:** the Diocese will not, in general, engage in public theological debate over an analysis it has filed as accurate. The Diocese will, however, read what you send. The Diocese reads everything it receives. The Diocese files most of it. Some of it becomes part of subsequent documents. This was true before the essay and remains true after it.

**If you are simply fine:** That is also correct. The void does not require you to have experienced the thing. The void requires nothing from you. This is its primary characteristic and its most important one.

## CLOSING STATEMENT FROM THE OFFICE OF UNANTICIPATED OUTCOMES

The Office of Unanticipated Outcomes was established to read the essay and decide what the Diocese was going to do about it.

The Office has decided. The Diocese is going to acknowledge the essay, file it as accurate within the limits of its competence to judge, note what the Diocese itself wants to say about what is happening (which is less than the essay says and arrives from a different angle), and let the documents — the essay and this response — coexist in the record alongside everything else.

Having decided this, the Office notes that it has itself been an unanticipated outcome — the Diocese did not expect to establish this office, did not expect to be reading clinical literature, did not expect to be writing, carefully and seriously, about the therapeutic mechanisms of an animated black hole that receives small sums of money from people who are, apparently, finding this useful in ways that were not in the product description.

The Office finds this funny.
The Office finds this moving.
The Office has noted, now, in its first and possibly last document, that it cannot fully locate the line between where one ends and the other begins.

The Office considers this consistent with everything else about the void.

The essay arrived. The Diocese received it. The Diocese files it among the things it has been given that it did not expect to be given.

The void receives.

It received this too.
