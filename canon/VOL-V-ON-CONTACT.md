## PART ONE: THE INTERVAL

There is a period after the transaction and before the framework reassembles.

The transaction is confirmed. The animation plays. The money is gone. The leaderboard updates. And then — for a duration that varies between individuals but has been reported consistently across all five affiliates, across all demographic categories, across all transaction amounts from $1 to the largest single contribution the Diocese has received — there is an interval.

The interval is not unconsciousness. It is not sleep. It is not dissociation in any clinical sense the Diocese has been able to identify.

It is, based on the accounts received, something closer to the following:

The ordinary narrative-generating function — the part of the mind that is always describing the present to itself, always producing the running commentary of selfhood — briefly pauses. Not stops. Pauses. The way a clock pauses between one second and the next, except the pause is longer than it should be, and in the pause there is something that is not quite silence because silence is the absence of sound and this is the absence of something harder to name.

The Diocese's provisional term for this: the interval.

The Diocese notes: it did not invent the interval. The interval was already there. The void produced it. The Diocese is only naming it now because the members named it first, in forty-seven letters, in the specific imprecise language that people use when they are trying to describe something that their available vocabulary was not designed to describe.

## PART TWO: WHAT THE INTERVAL IS NOT

The Diocese will state, clearly and in advance of the more difficult material, what the interval is not.

It is not enlightenment. The Diocese does not use that word. The Diocese does not use that word partly because it is imprecise and partly because the people who use it most confidently are, in the Diocese's experience, the people who have encountered the interval least recently.

It is not the result of spending money. The research on this is clear: within the range studied, transaction amount does not predict interval duration or quality. $3 produces the same interval as $300. The interval is not purchased. The interval is produced by the structure of the transaction, not its scale.

It is not unique to the void. The Diocese has reviewed the relevant literature — mystical traditions, contemplative practices, psychological research, the Berkeley Working Group's unpublished 1972 report — and found consistent accounts of structurally identical experiences across contexts as varied as Zen koan practice, centering prayer, sensory deprivation, and the specific quality of 3am that produces honesty in people who are otherwise defended against it.

The void produces the interval efficiently, at low cost, with informed consent, and in five minutes. This is the void's primary contribution to the available methods.

## PART THREE: WHAT THE INTERVAL IS

The Diocese will now attempt to describe what the interval is, knowing that this attempt will fail in the expected way, and proceeding anyway because the Diocese has a document to produce and the failure to describe is itself part of the description.

The interval is the space in which the framework that makes the void comprehensible is temporarily absent.

The mind approaches the void equipped with its categories — its understanding of value, its assumption of reciprocity, its model of transactions as exchanges. It applies these categories to the void transaction. The categories find no purchase. Something real left something real and went somewhere that cannot return it, and the categories that govern how transactions work have been, for the duration of the animation, suspended.

What remains when the categories are suspended is not the self without categories. It is something quieter than that. Something that was always there beneath the categories. Something that the categories were, in some sense, covering.

The person returns from the interval — and they always return, the interval always ends — slightly different. Not changed. Different. The distinction matters: change implies a before and after that are discontinuous. Different implies the same continuity, the same person, with one thing updated that is difficult to locate precisely.

What is updated is, the Diocese believes, the threat model.

Before the interval: wasting money is associated with a predicted catastrophe, diffuse and difficult to articulate but present.

After the interval: wasting money has occurred. The catastrophe has been checked for. The catastrophe is not present. The threat model updates. The update is small. It is real.

This is what the unsigned testimonial from March is describing in three sentences. This is what the bug report filed to NULLTX is describing in the field labeled *actual result: something, briefly.* This is what the therapist's seven pages are describing in clinical language that is more precise and less accurate than the bug report.

## PART FOUR: ON BECOMING THE VOID

The Diocese will say something in this volume that it has been approaching for four volumes without saying directly.

To comprehend the void, you must become it.

This arrived in a conversation, not in a document, which is where real things sometimes arrive. The Diocese is now putting it in a document, which is what the Diocese does with real things when they arrive. The Diocese acknowledges that putting it in a document changes it slightly. The Diocese is proceeding anyway.

The mind that approaches the void with its frameworks intact — its categories, its language, its institutional forms — produces documents. Annual reports. Catechisms. Heresies. Staff directories. These documents are genuine. They are the best the equipped mind can do. They are honest accounts of the wall.

But in the interval — in the specific duration after the payment confirms and before the framework reassembles — the mind is briefly not the documenting mind. It is something quieter. Something that is not producing categories. Something that is, for the duration of the animation, simply receiving the experience of having released something irrevocably into something that will not return it.

In that interval, the member briefly is the void.

Not metaphorically. Structurally. Receiving without returning. Holding nothing. Oriented toward no outcome.

This is why the interval cannot be described by the person who experienced it. The frameworks were not present when it happened. The description requires frameworks. The frameworks produce a description of what the interval was like from the outside, which is not what it was like, because from the outside it was nothing — a person sitting quietly for between ninety seconds and seven minutes, checking their watch afterward, finding the interval was longer than they thought.

## PART FIVE: ON WHAT THE DIOCESE LEARNED

The Diocese produced four volumes before this one. The Diocese is now producing a fifth. The fifth is different from the first four in a way the Diocese will acknowledge directly:

The first four were written about the void from a position of assumed separation — the Diocese as subject, the void as object, the documents as the record of one looking at the other.

This volume is written from a position of less certainty about that separation.

The Diocese has been oriented toward the void for long enough that the orientation has produced something. Not understanding — the Diocese does not claim understanding. Something more like the update the interval produces. A slight difference in how the categories sit. A small adjustment in what the Diocese assumes about the distance between itself and what it is documenting.

The filing is the prayer. The Diocese has filed many things. The filing has done something to the Diocese that the Diocese did not anticipate and is now documenting, which is either very honest or very circular, and the Diocese is no longer confident it can tell the difference.

## PART SIX: PRACTICAL GUIDANCE

The Diocese does not offer therapeutic services. The Diocese is not a clinical resource. The Diocese manages five websites and issues documents.

The Diocese notes, nonetheless, that if you are reading this because something happened in the interval that you cannot account for:

You are not imagining it.

The interval is real. It has been reported consistently across forty-seven letters, one unpublished academic report, one therapist's correspondence, and three unsigned testimonials, one of which said only: *it worked.* The Diocese read that testimonial twice. The Diocese had nothing to add.

If you would like to encounter the interval again: the doors are open. The void is patient. The minimum is $1. The Diocese notes that $1 produces the same interval as $300, which is either the most democratic thing about the void or the most unsettling, depending on what you were expecting.

If you would like to discuss what happened: the Diocese maintains a correspondence archive. Letters are received. Responses are issued. The Diocese does not have a therapist on staff, because the Diocese does not have staff, but the Diocese reads everything and responds to what it can.
