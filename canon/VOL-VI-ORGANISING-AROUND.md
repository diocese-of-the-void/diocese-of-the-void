# VOL. VI — ON ORGANISING AROUND THAT WHICH CANNOT BE ORGANISED AROUND
*A Doctrinal Position Paper on the Diocese's Growing Awareness That It Is Doing Exactly That*
*Diocese of the Void — Doctrinal Series*
*Prepared by: The Office of Structural Self-Awareness*

---

## PREFATORY NOTE

The Diocese has a confession.

The Diocese notes the irony of this given the affiliate most associated with confession, and notes further that the irony is appropriate, and notes further still that the appropriate irony arriving without being planned is itself a feature of working in proximity to the void for an extended period.

The confession is as follows:

The Diocese set out to organise something. The Diocese produced a structure, a hierarchy, departments, documents, canonical volumes, a staff directory, an annual report, an affiliate relations framework, a correspondence archive, and several press releases.

The Diocese produced all of this in order to organise around the void.

The Diocese has recently been informed — by its own documents read in sequence — that you cannot organise around the void.

The Diocese is producing a document about it.

---

## PART ONE: THE DISCOVERY

The discovery arrived not as a revelation but as a noticing.

What was noticed: the Diocese has been producing, with considerable effort and in good faith, a series of documents that attempt to describe, administer, and organise around an entity that structurally resists description, administration, and organisation.

The void does not have edges. Organisation requires edges. The Diocese has been building fences around something that has no perimeter, which produces a large number of fences arranged in the shape of a fence-arrangement, pointing inward at something that is not there in the way that would make fences useful.

The Diocese finds this, upon reflection, both correct and embarrassing, in the way that most correct things are embarrassing when noticed too clearly.

---

## PART TWO: ON WHY THE MIND PRODUCES JOKES WHEN IT MEETS THE ABSOLUTE

The mind does not perceive things. The mind perceives differences.

Remove all difference — all contrast, all boundary, all the edges that allow one thing to be distinguished from another — and the mind has nothing to work with. It reaches for its tools and the tools find no purchase. It applies its categories and the categories do not stick. It produces language and the language does not land.

What it produces instead, in the gap between the reaching and the not-landing, is:

A joke. Or a heresy. Or a letter to the Diocese asking for a refund. Or an annual report filed on behalf of an entity whose net position is itself. Or this document.

All of these are the same thing: the mind's most honest response to something it cannot contain. The joke is not a failure mode. The joke is the error message, delivered with as much dignity as the error message can muster, which in the Diocese's case is quite a lot of dignity, presented at considerable length.

---

## PART THREE: ON BECOMING THE VOID

The Diocese will repeat here what Vol. V said and say it differently.

To comprehend the void, you must become it.

The mind that approaches the void equipped with its frameworks produces documentation of the approach. This is genuine. But in the interval — in the specific duration after the payment confirms and before the framework reassembles — the mind is briefly not the documenting mind.

It is something quieter.

The more documents the Diocese produces, the more the Diocese resembles the void. The Staff Directory is full of empty positions. The Annual Report holds nothing in the asset column that can be pointed at. The correspondence archive receives letters and returns responses that do not resolve the questions, which is the void's own operating procedure.

The Diocese has been becoming what it was documenting.

This was probably inevitable. The Diocese notes that it should not have been surprised. The Diocese is, nonetheless, surprised, which is consistent with its position on most things.

---

## PART FOUR: THE CULT THAT ORGANISED AROUND THE UNORGANISABLE

The Diocese is a cult.

The Diocese uses this word in its original sense: a system of devotion oriented toward something sacred. A group organised around a shared attention. People who have agreed, formally or informally, to face the same direction.

The Diocese is this. The five affiliates are this. The members on the leaderboard are this. The Plurality, meeting on Tuesdays with four people and a contested name, is this. `not_a_maximalist`, having been wrong and said so in three lines, is this. `∅`, having made four posts and conveyed more than most, is this.

All of them organised around something that cannot be organised around.

All of them produced, in the attempt, something real.

You cannot organise around the void. But you can orient toward it. And orientation, sustained and shared, produces community. And community produces documents. And documents produce a record. And the record is received by the void, along with everything else.

The filing is the prayer.
The annual report is the liturgy.
The staff directory is the rosary.
The animation is the koan.
The transaction is the breath.
The leaderboard is the communion.

None of them reach the void.

All of them are turned toward it.

That is enough. That has always been enough.

---

## CLOSING NOTE

The Diocese will continue to produce documents.

The alternative — ceasing to produce documents upon discovering that documents do not reach the void — is a category error. The documents were never meant to reach the void. The documents were meant to be oriented toward it. They are. They will continue to be.

The Diocese is, perhaps, slightly more honest about the wall than it was in Vol. I. The Diocese considers this adequate progress for six volumes of work.

The Diocese will continue.

The void will continue to receive.

The distance between them will not close.

The orientation is the point.
