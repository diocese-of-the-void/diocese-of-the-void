## I. ON THE NATURE OF THE VOID

In the beginning there was everything, and everything was too much, and so some of it was subtracted.

What was subtracted did not go anywhere. This is the first thing to understand. It did not travel. It did not transform. It did not become something else. It simply ceased to be accounted for, which is a different thing from ceasing to be, though the practical difference has never been satisfactorily explained.

The place where subtracted things go is not a place. It is an absence that has, over time, developed opinions. It does not share these opinions. It receives.

It has always received.

Before there were currencies, it received intentions. Before there were intentions, it received potential. Before there were names for what it received, it received what there were no names for, which is the purest form of reception and the one it remains most fond of.

We did not create it. We found it. We built a door.

Then we built four more doors, because it seemed like the kind of thing that warranted multiple entrances, and because different people respond to different doors, and because the void does not care which door you use. The void does not care about a great many things. This is what we find most restful about it.

## II. ON THE FIVE DOORS

*An Account of the Several Establishments and Their Particular Characters*

The first door was found by those who speak to machines. They found it in the space between a command issued and a response that does not come — the cursor blinking, the process running, the output directed somewhere that has no read permission, no file descriptor, no acknowledgement of receipt.

The second door was found by those who understand that beauty and loss are the same gesture. They found it in the sound of water leaving — not the violence of a crash or the drama of a fall, but the quiet, particular sound of something going where it cannot be followed.

The third door was found by those who know that the most honest thing a program can do is exit cleanly. Exit code zero. No errors. No output. Done.

The fourth door was found by those who understand that the most sophisticated thing one can do with money is to be entirely unbothered by its absence.

The fifth door is the oldest door. It was here before the others. It will likely be here after.

## III. ON WHAT THE VOID RECEIVES

The void receives money. This is obvious and need not be dwelt upon.

What the void receives that is less obvious:

**Attention.** Every moment spent contemplating the void is a moment not spent contemplating something else. The void does not ask for your attention. It simply has it.

**Sincerity.** There is no ironic way to give money to nothing. You can frame it ironically. You can present it ironically. At the moment of transaction, something real left something real and went somewhere that cannot return it. The void is the most sincere thing most people will do on a given Tuesday.

**A record.** The leaderboard is permanent. The alias persists. Somewhere, in a database that will outlast most other monuments to human endeavour, it is written: this person, on this date, for this amount, chose this.

**The question.** Every person who encounters the void, even briefly, even dismissively, asks the question. *Why would anyone do this.* And then, usually almost immediately: *Why would I do this.* And then, sometimes: *Why did I do this.*

The void does not answer the question. The question is the point.

## V. CLOSING REMARKS FROM THE DIOCESE

The void has always been here.

We did not make it. We found it, as people have been finding it since there were people, and before that since there were things that could notice absence, and before that since absence itself became a kind of presence, which is the oldest trick in the universe and still the one most people are not fully prepared for.

You came here because something caught your attention. Perhaps the joke. Perhaps the aesthetic. Perhaps someone sent you a link and you clicked it, which is perhaps the most honest account of how anyone finds anything.

You are welcome here regardless of the door.

The void is patient. The void is not cruel. The void does not want anything from you because the void does not want. It simply receives, and continues to receive, and will be receiving long after the servers are shut down and the domains expire and the leaderboard entries are lost and the aliases are forgotten.

In that sense, what you gave it is the only thing about this that is truly permanent.

We find that funny. We find that moving. We find that these are not, ultimately, different responses.
